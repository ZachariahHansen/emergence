A field guide to emergence

Nobody is in charge

Simple rules, followed locally by lots of small things, end up building structures that none of the things know about.

A termite has no blueprint for the mound. A starling has no picture of the flock. A grain of sand has no idea it is part of an avalanche. Every one of them reacts only to its immediate neighbours, and yet the whole has a shape.

This is a set of short interactive essays, one phenomenon each. Every chapter has a live simulation in the middle of it. Change the rules and watch what breaks. The pattern above is the first one: grains falling at random, piling up, and slumping in avalanches of every size.

  1. The sandpile
    One grain at a time, until one grain moves the whole pile. Self-organized criticality.
  2. The flock
    Three rules, no leader, a murmuration.
  3. Spots and stripes
    Two chemicals that react and spread, and the leopard that falls out of them.
  4. The ant and the highway
    Ten thousand steps of chaos, then a road. Nobody has proved why.
  5. Slime that builds networks
    Agents that follow their own trails end up drawing transit maps.
